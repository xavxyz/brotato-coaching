What a point of a stat is worth

Lesson 1 of the spine. Why the stat screen cannot tell you whether to buy a stat, and what can. Four minutes, four questions.

Brotato 1.1.12.0.beta-3 · 79 weapon families

The one sentence

A stat is worth whatever the weapons in your hands say it is worth — nothing, if they do not list it — so the question in the shop is never "is this stat good?" but "what does my weapon do with it?"

This is the wave-9 problem. Forty materials, three offers, twenty seconds of thinking, and a stat screen that tells you what you would own rather than what it would do. The number that answers the question is not on that screen: it is on the weapon.

The model, in three lines

  1. Every weapon has a scaling list, and it is short. A stat that is not on the list does nothing at all for that weapon. Melee Damage, the most common one, appears on only 164 of the 258 weapon entries. Lists extracted from the installed game; reading them, STAT-S2.
  2. A listed stat is multiplied by that weapon's own coefficient, then added. Knife IV is base 25 at ×0.8 Melee, so 30 Melee Damage makes it 25 + 24 = 49 a hit. The coefficient is the whole story: on Ranged Damage, Obliterator IV takes ×15 and SMG IV takes ×0.8, from the same shop and the same point. Coefficients extracted; STAT-S2 for the notation.
  3. % Damage multiplies what those two produced, so it is worth little early and a lot once the flat part is large. Flat first, percentage after — and the crossover is arithmetic you can do in the shop. STAT-S2, STAT-S3.

The trap is the label. Melee and ranged are cosmetic for scaling: Harpoon Gun IV is a ranged weapon that scales with Ranged (×1) and Melee (×1). Read the list, never the label.

Now answer from memory

Say each answer out loud, or write it, before you ask for the options. Producing an answer is what survives to wave 12; recognising one in a list is not, and the options below are deliberately the same length so that nothing but knowing the material picks one.

  1. You are holding two Knife IVs and nothing else. The shop offers +3 Ranged Damage. What does buying it add to your damage?

    • Knife IV lists Melee Damage and nothing else, so Ranged Damage is worth exactly zero to it — however good the stat looks on the screen.

    • This is the stat screen's answer, not the weapon's. A flat stat only adds through a coefficient on a weapon that lists it.

    • 3 × 0.8 is the right arithmetic on the wrong stat: 0.8 is the Knife's coefficient for Melee Damage.

    • Not "small" — zero. An unlisted stat has no coefficient at all, so there is no small amount for it to be worth.

  2. Twenty Ranged Damage adds +300 a hit to Obliterator IV, at ×15. SMG IV takes the same stat at ×0.8. What do those twenty points add to the SMG?

    • 20 × 0.8. A point of Ranged Damage is not an amount of damage; it is whatever the weapon holding it says, and here the spread is twentyfold.

    • That is the Obliterator's gain. Coefficients belong to weapons, not to stats, so the same purchase pays differently in each hand.

    • No averaging happens: the SMG's own coefficient is applied, and it is one of the smallest in the catalogue.

    • Closer to the truth than three hundred, but the coefficient multiplies the stat, not the other way around: 0.8 × 20, not 20 ÷ 0.8.

  3. Your Knife IV sits at 30 Melee Damage — 49 a hit. The shop offers +5 Melee Damage, or +10% Damage. Which one is bigger this wave?

    • +5 × 0.8 = +4, so 53. Close, but second — and it would have won at a smaller base, which is the crossover worth carrying.

    • 10% multiplies the whole 49, for +4.9. Percentages grow with the flat base beneath them, so they overtake it as the run goes on.

    • Four against 4.9 — near enough to make the point that the answer moves, but not a tie at this base.

    • % Damage multiplies what you already have, and at 49 damage a hit there is not yet much to multiply.

  4. One check decides whether a stat on offer is worth anything to you at all, before any arithmetic. Which check is it?

    • The transferable move, and it works on a character you have never played: read the scaling lists in your hands, then the coefficients, then buy.

    • It always goes higher. That is the failure mode this lesson exists to break.

    • A character's modifiers say what is cheap for it to reach, not what your current weapons convert into damage. The weapon is the first check.

    • Price ranks purchases that are worth something. It cannot rescue a stat worth zero.
